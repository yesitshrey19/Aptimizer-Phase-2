IS 456:2000 — Plain and Reinforced Concrete, Code of Practice (checked facts)
NOTE — Values summarised in Aptimizer's own words from IS 456:2000 (with Amendment No. 1), checked by Saurav Ambasta in Phase II testing against the PDF copy of the standard. Page numbers are PDF page numbers of that copy. This is not the text of the Code. Only values that were checked are listed.

Table 5 Minimum grade, cement content and water-cement ratio for reinforced concrete
For reinforced concrete under IS 456:2000 Table 5: mild exposure needs a minimum grade of M20; moderate exposure needs M25, a minimum cement content of 300 kg/m³ and a maximum free water-cement ratio of 0.50; severe exposure needs M30, a minimum cement content of 320 kg/m³ and a maximum water-cement ratio of 0.45; extreme exposure needs M40, 360 kg/m³ and 0.40. Checked on PDF page 33.

Table 16 Nominal cover to meet durability requirements
Under IS 456:2000 Table 16 (read with Cl. 26.4.2), the nominal concrete cover to all reinforcement is 20 mm for mild exposure, 30 mm for moderate exposure and 45 mm for severe exposure. Checked on PDF page 60.

26.4.2.1 Nominal cover for longitudinal reinforcing bars in a column
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 26.4.2.1, the nominal cover to the longitudinal bars of a column shall be not less than 40 mm and not less than the diameter of the bar. Checked on PDF page 59.

26.4.2.2 Nominal cover for footings
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 26.4.2.2, the minimum nominal cover to the reinforcement of footings is 50 mm. This is higher than the cover for columns (40 mm) because footings are cast against soil and are more exposed to moisture. Checked on PDF page 59.

36.4.2 Partial safety factors for material strength
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 36.4.2, the partial safety factor for material strength at the limit state of collapse is 1.5 for concrete and 1.15 for steel. Checked on PDF page 81.

Table 18 Values of partial safety factor for loads
Under IS 456:2000 Table 18, at the limit state of collapse the load factor is 1.5 for dead load plus imposed load (DL + LL), and 1.2 for dead load plus imposed load plus earthquake or wind load (DL + LL + EL). Checked on PDF pages 81 to 82.

25.1.2 Short and slender compression members
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 25.1.2, a column is treated as a short column when the ratio of its effective length to its least lateral dimension is less than 12; otherwise it is slender. Checked on PDF pages 53 to 54.

25.4 Minimum eccentricity
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 25.4, every column shall be designed for a minimum eccentricity of l/500 + D/30 (unsupported length over 500 plus lateral dimension over 30), subject to a minimum of 20 mm. Checked on PDF page 55.

26.5.3.1 Longitudinal reinforcement in columns
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 26.5.3.1, the longitudinal steel in a column shall be not less than 0.8 percent and not more than 6 percent of the gross cross-sectional area (4 percent is recommended as a practical maximum). A rectangular column needs at least 4 longitudinal bars and a circular column at least 6, and the bars shall be not less than 12 mm in diameter. Checked on PDF page 61.

26.5.3.2 Transverse reinforcement in columns
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 26.5.3.2(c), the pitch of lateral ties shall not exceed the least of: the least lateral dimension of the column, 16 times the smallest longitudinal bar diameter, and 300 mm. The diameter of a lateral tie shall be not less than one-fourth of the diameter of the largest longitudinal bar and not less than 6 mm; the 6 mm figure is from Amendment No. 1 (PDF page 12), which replaced the 16 mm printed in the original clause. Checked on PDF page 62.

23.2.1 Basic values of span to effective depth
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 23.2.1, for spans up to 10 m the basic span to effective depth ratios are 7 for cantilevers, 20 for simply supported members and 26 for continuous members. Checked on PDF page 50.

23.2 Deflection limits
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 23.2(a), the final deflection of a beam or slab, including the effects of temperature, creep and shrinkage, shall not exceed span/250. Checked on PDF page 50.

26.5.1.1 Tension reinforcement in beams
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 26.5.1.1, the minimum tension reinforcement in a beam is given by As/(b·d) = 0.85/fy, and the maximum tension reinforcement shall not exceed 0.04 bD. Checked on PDF page 60.

26.5.2.1 Minimum reinforcement in slabs
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 26.5.2.1, the reinforcement in either direction in a slab shall be not less than 0.12 percent of the gross cross-sectional area with high strength deformed (HYSD) bars, or 0.15 percent with mild steel bars. Checked on PDF page 61.

26.5.2.2 Maximum diameter of bars in slabs
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 26.5.2.2, the diameter of reinforcing bars in a slab shall not exceed one-eighth of the total thickness of the slab. Checked on PDF page 61.

26.3.3 Maximum spacing of bars in slabs
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 26.3.3(b), the main bars in a slab shall be spaced not more than 3 times the effective depth or 300 mm, whichever is smaller; distribution bars not more than 5 times the effective depth or 450 mm, whichever is smaller. Checked on PDF page 59.

39.3 Short axially loaded members in compression
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 39.3, the design axial load of a short, axially loaded column is Pu = 0.4 fck Ac + 0.67 fy Asc, where fck is the characteristic concrete strength, Ac the area of concrete, fy the characteristic strength of the steel and Asc the area of longitudinal steel. Checked on PDF page 84.

6.2.2 Flexural strength of concrete
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 6.2.2, the flexural strength of concrete may be taken as fcr = 0.7 √fck N/mm², where fck is the characteristic cube compressive strength of the concrete in N/mm². It is used for cracking and deflection checks. Checked on PDF page 29.

6.2.3.1 Modulus of elasticity of concrete
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 6.2.3.1, the short-term static modulus of elasticity of concrete may be taken as Ec = 5000 √fck N/mm², where fck is the characteristic cube compressive strength in N/mm². Actual measured values may differ from this estimate. Checked on PDF page 29.

13.5.1 Curing
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 13.5.1, exposed concrete made with ordinary Portland cement shall be kept moist for at least 7 days; with mineral admixtures or blended cements, or in dry and hot weather, the period is at least 10 days. Checked on PDF page 40.

11.3.1 Stripping time of formwork
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 11.3.1, with ordinary Portland cement, props to slabs spanning up to 4.5 m may be removed after 7 days, and props to beams spanning up to 6 m after 14 days. Checked on PDF page 38.

26.2.1 Development length of bars
Under IS 456:2000 Cl. 26.2.1, the development length is Ld = φ σs / (4 τbd), where φ is the bar diameter, σs the stress in the bar at the design load and τbd the design bond stress. Checked on PDF page 55.
